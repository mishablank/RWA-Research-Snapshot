Consensus

On the facts of today, the desks roughly converge.#

Strip out the forecasts and there's a shared picture: a small, fast-growing market that is institutionalizing, anchored by tokenized Treasuries, and sitting at the very bottom of its adoption curve.

~0.01%

What every desk signs up to
  • Institutional issuers now lead growth – the market has shifted from crypto-native protocols to banks, asset managers and transfer agents.
  • Treasuries, private credit and gold are the live categories – equities are inflecting from a tiny base (sub-$300M → ~$1.7B · +177% H1, the fastest-growing category).
  • Market infrastructure is going to production in 2026 – DTCC, Nasdaq and major custodians are moving from pilots to live rails.
  • The market is highly concentrated – only U.S. Treasuries have reached "production grade"; ~62 assets hold 88% of value and five products are half of it (BeInCrypto × rwa.xyz).
The shared catalysts
  • U.S. stablecoin law (GENIUS Act) – cited by nearly every desk as the unlock for Treasury-backed on-chain dollars.
  • DTCC tokenization service – limited production July 2026, broader launch October 2026.
  • Stablecoins have hit payments scale – adjusted volume past Visa; $200B+ of Treasuries now sit inside reserves.
The one number nobody disputes

Tokenized assets are roughly one basis point of their addressable market. The argument is entirely about the slope from here.

BeInCrypto frames the market in three layers – ~$60B of core tokenized assets, a ~$695B full settlement layer, and ~$8T of monthly on-chain settlement volume (Apr 2026, +268% YoY). Different layer, different number – which is precisely the point.

New in August: Standard Chartered's ~$340B lands within 3% of 21Shares' ~$350B – and the two numbers share almost nothing. 21Shares gets there by adding permissioned networks to a $31B public-chain base; SC gets there by adding stablecoins. Same total, different universe – the denominator problem in one coincidence.

The takeaway

The 36× gap is definitional, not factual. Free-float vs. represented, public-only vs. plus-permissioned, core assets vs. the full settlement layer. Always check the denominator before you trust the forecast.

Discrepancy 03 · is the base layer money?06 / 10
Stablecoins – foundation or fault line

The rail one camp builds on is the risk another wants to contain.#

No disagreement is sharper. To crypto desks, stablecoins are the settlement layer for tokenization. To the central-banking world, they're a flawed form of money to be superseded.

Crypto research
Stablecoins are the rail.

Binance: $200B+ of Treasuries already sit inside stablecoin reserves; adjusted volume has passed Visa and is nearing ACH. They're the on-ramp, settlement layer and the largest RWA category by reserves – the foundation everything else is built on.

Binance Research · 2026
Banks & consultancies
A three-layer coexistence.

McKinsey, Deutsche Bank and GFMA frame a stack – stablecoins, tokenized bank deposits and central-bank money side by side, each for different uses. McKinsey models ~$4T of stablecoin adoption by 2030 alongside >$4T/yr in tokenized deposits.

McKinsey · Deutsche Bank · GFMA · 2026
Official sector
A flawed form of money.

The BIS argues stablecoins fail on singleness, elasticity and integrity, and that the future is tokenized central-bank + commercial-bank money on a "unified ledger" (Project Agorá) – not private stablecoins at scale.

BIS Annual Economic Report 2026, Ch. III

Even within the "for" camp there's nuance: Standard Chartered notes USD stablecoins hold ~98% share and asks where non-USD coins can realistically scale.

Discrepancy 05 · whose chain08 / 10
Public vs. permissioned rails

The biggest pool of tokenized value may be the one crypto can't see.#

The trackers everyone quotes count public chains. But the banks are issuing on permissioned ledgers – and by at least one estimate that hidden layer is an order of magnitude larger.

↗

The public-chain bet

Standard Chartered projects ~$4T of tokenized assets on public blockchains by 2028 – reaffirmed in August's "Owning the rails", which frames oracle and interoperability infrastructure as the layer that decides it. Crypto trackers (RWA.xyz, DefiLlama, CoinGecko) measure this layer.

⌗

The permissioned layer

21Shares puts ~$31B on public chains but ~$350B once permissioned networks like Canton are included. Banks (J.P. Morgan Kinexys, Citi via DTCC) issue largely on permissioned rails that public dashboards never see.

The implication

If most institutional issuance lands on permissioned ledgers, the public-chain dashboards undercount the market ~11× – and "RWA growth" charts measure the wrong thing.

Discrepancy 06 · what unlocks it09 / 10
The binding constraint

Five desks, five different bottlenecks.#

Ask what's actually holding tokenization back and you get a different answer depending on who's writing – which tells you the market is constrained on several fronts at once.

01

Citi → market infrastructure

The unlock is incumbents embedding tokenization: DTCC production (Jul/Oct 2026), NYSE and Nasdaq pathways.

02

Standard Chartered → data & interop rails

Oracle and interoperability infrastructure is the layer that decides who captures tokenized flow – the argument of August's "Owning the rails".

03

Binance → convergence

No single lever – regulation, custody, settlement, liquidity and distribution have to reinforce each other.

04

Banks → regulatory clarity

GENIUS Act (stablecoins), CLARITY Act (market structure) and MiCA define what institutions can actually issue.

05

IMF → safety & coordination

Safe settlement assets, legal certainty and international coordination first – a notably more cautious framing.

06

BIS → monetary architecture

The real prerequisite is a unified ledger anchored in central-bank money – not better stablecoins.

07

The shared clock

One catalyst nearly everyone names: DTCC's production launch in October 2026.

The takeaway10 / 10
So what

The disagreements are about scope and clock, not direction.#

Normalize the definitions and the desks tell one story: penetration is ~0.01%, Treasuries lead today, stablecoins carry the settlement layer, and the next 12–18 months set the slope. The 35× forecast spread is mostly an artifact of three choices – what counts as an RWA, public vs. permissioned, and whether stablecoins are in.

What to watch – the tie-breakers
  • DTCC production launch – October 2026; tokenization inside default U.S. market plumbing.
  • GENIUS Act implementation – reserve & disclosure rules that decide stablecoin-Treasury demand.
  • Permissioned-network disclosure – if Canton & bank ledgers report, the "true" size resets.
  • First sovereign on-chain issuance – UK DIGIT, Japan pilots moving to commercial scale.
How to read any RWA report
  • Check the denominator first – free-float, represented, or all-in incl. stablecoins?
  • Ask which chains it counts – public-only numbers can undercount ~11×.
  • Separate stock from revenue – "$X trillion of assets" and "$Y billion of revenue" are different claims.
  • Treat 2030 figures as scenarios – the base/bull range is usually wider than the point estimate suggests.
Bottom line

The market isn't arguing about whether – it's arguing about how you count and how fast. Pin those two down and most of the contradiction disappears.
